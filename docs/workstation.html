Options

the interface, the model, its limits, and why there is no chain
no chain No source ingested by this project publishes an option chain for CBOT, CME or ICE contracts. yfinance serves equity option chains only, and none of the twenty-five data layers here carries a strike, a premium or an implied volatility. Rather than render an empty ladder — which would read as 'no options traded' — the chain is reported unavailable and the manual-input workflow is offered instead.
Manual workflow. Enter the premium or the implied volatility your broker quoted, together with the option's expiry date and a discount rate, and the Black-76 value and Greeks will be computed from those inputs and labelled as model values.
A desk can enter a broker's premium or implied volatility by hand, or import an exported ladder, and get Black-76 values and Greeks from it. Those quotes are the client's own records and are rendered only to the private edition.
Model. black76 — assumptions, all of which bite:
  • underlying is a futures price, lognormal with one constant volatility to expiry
  • European exercise; listed grain options are American and the early-exercise premium is not modelled
  • constant continuously-compounded discount rate, supplied as an input
  • time to expiry in years on a 365-day calendar, to the option's own expiration

Where Black-76 is wrong about these options

AssumptionWhat a listed grain option doesConsequenceBites
the option is European and can only be exercised at expiry CBOT options on grain futures are American — the holder may exercise into the underlying future on any business day up to expiration the right to exercise early can only be worth something or nothing, never less than nothing, so the Black-76 number is a floor for an American option rather than a value for one. The gap widens for deep in-the-money options and for puts when rates are high understates
one constant volatility describes every strike and the whole life grain implied volatility smiles, and the smile steepens sharply into a weather market — out-of-the-money calls in a drought carry a volatility several points above the at-the-money pricing the wings off an at-the-money volatility understates them; using a wing volatility at the money overstates it. A single number is right in at most one place on the ladder either
volatility is constant from today to expiration grain volatility is seasonal and event-driven — a WASDE, a pollination window or a South American planting scare raises it on a known calendar an option spanning a known event is worth more than a flat-vol model says, and one expiring before it is worth less; the model cannot tell them apart either
a single continuously-compounded rate holds to expiry the rate is an input this project does not source; the caller chooses it the effect on a short-dated grain option is small, but it is not zero and it is not measured here — rho is reported per rate point so the choice stays visible either
time to expiry runs on a 365-day calendar of continuous trading the market trades on business days and volatility does not accrue evenly over weekends and exchange holidays a short-dated option across a long holiday weekend is overstated on a calendar-day clock; the difference is largest in the last fortnight of an option's life overstates
the inputs describe a traded option no source this project ingests publishes an option premium, a strike ladder or an implied volatility for any of these contracts every number here came from a person or a file they supplied, and is a model value computed from it — it is never a market observation unknown
Model values below discount at a stated 4.0% continuously-compounded rate. It is a constant on this page, not a sourced number — rho on each row shows what a different rate would be worth.
